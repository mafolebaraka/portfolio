# Community challenge design

How I structure a 28 or 31 day engagement challenge so participation stays high to the last day. Built and refined across #28DaysWithGrey, #31DaysOfGettingaNewClient, #31DaysOfBeingFinanciallyIndependent and #TheGreyAreaAugChallenge in The Grey Area, and reused for Remotika Africa cohorts.

## One theme, four weeks

Pick a single theme that serves the product or mission without selling it. A month themed on saving is a warm up for a savings feature. A month themed on getting a new client serves freelancers directly. Break the theme into four weekly sub themes so each week has its own arc.

## Mix the interaction types

The mistake is a month of text prompts. Participation drops by day five. Instead, plan a deliberate mix:

* **Polls** for quick opinions and to surface data you can reuse in content
* **Emoji only replies** for the lowest possible barrier
* **Tag a member days** to pull in lurkers through their friends
* **Comment prompts** with one clear question
* **GIF and photo days** for personality and fun
* **Text reflection prompts** reserved for the last few days of each week or the end of the month, when people are warmed up and have something to say

Lower barrier formats drive breadth. Text prompts earn depth only after breadth.

## Write it in bulk, schedule it once

Draft all 28 or 31 prompts before day one. Each prompt is written as a person talking to members, using "we" and "us" and acknowledging real struggles, not as a brand template.

## Segment the communications

Previous challenge participants get a different DM from new members. Winner notifications read like a real person typing: short, warm, human.

## Recognise people every week

A weekly "stars" post naming top contributors, and a member of the month at the end. Recognition is the retention loop.

## Measure

Daily participation count, unique participants for the month, the share of participants who were inactive the month before (the activation number), and which formats produced the most replies. Use the last two to design the next challenge.
