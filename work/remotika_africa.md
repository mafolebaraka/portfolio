# Remotika Africa

**Role:** Founder and Digital Lead, 2024 to present
**Sites:** [remotikaafrica.org](https://www.remotikaafrica.org) · [blog.remotika.org](https://blog.remotika.org) · [space.remotikaafrica.org](https://space.remotikaafrica.org)
**Stack:** WordPress, FluentCommunity, FluentCRM, Fluent Forms, FluentCart with Flutterwave, Plunk, Flodesk, Substack, YouTube, Spotify and Apple Podcasts

## Context

Remotika Africa is a freelancing and remote work education movement for young Tanzanians and East Africans. The promise is simple: identify your digital skillset, learn it, monetize it. It is explicitly not a hiring platform, and we say so in every audience communication. I founded it after my Grey community role and built it with my own funds together with youth from my digital skilling programmes.

Three audiences: complete beginners, career shifters and side hustlers.

## What I built

**Cohort programme.** A 12 week freelancing curriculum built on the Thriving in the Gig Economy framework, with timetable, student communications, welcome sequences, cohort update emails and a referral outreach programme. Cohorts 1 and 2 ran in 2025 and 2026; Cohorts 3 and 4 of the Freelancing Masterclass sell at TSh 150,000 each, with a Meta Ads course at TSh 75,000, all paywalled inside the community.

**Community.** Migrated the community to FluentCommunity in June 2026 with 11 spaces: a lounge plus expert spaces for the online money academy, financial education, digital marketing school, tax education and mental health. Courses, a leaderboard and two free tools (a freelancing rate calculator and an invoice and quote generator).

**Email.** Grew and segmented a Flodesk list into 25 segments (Circle members, Instagram audience, masterclass attendees, Upwork sales students, remote jobs list, event attendees, cohort interest forms and more), then audited the analytics: engagement diluted as send volume scaled, and Swahili language sends were the top performers. Later migrated all contacts (WordPress users, FluentCRM contacts and Fluent Forms submissions) to Plunk.

**Events.** A July 2026 in person event in Dar es Salaam, followed by a post event newsletter with feedback form and photo drive. Next: a 1,000+ freelancer event around 31 October 2026, with the email list getting first access.

**Interns.** Drafted and recruited seven unpaid remote intern roles for Cohort 2 (content distribution, clipping, podcast editing, YouTube management, graphic design, project management, Swahili article writing), each with its own application form and a one role only rule.

**Partnerships and monetisation.** A brand partnerships rate card in TZS and USD covering Substack ad placement, community site ads, email sends, hosted virtual events and physical events. Partner offers such as a YouTube Automation masterclass at a member discount. Recruitment vendor work for client data collection projects, with forms that hide client details from participants and double as a community join CTA.

**Website.** Rebuilt the homepage in September 2026 as a single landing page focused only on the free community and paid courses, keeping the purple and yellow brand.

## Results (dated)

* **13,198 community members** with **4,387 active in the last 90 days**, 1,123 posts and 3,517 comments between May and August 2026, within about three months of the FluentCommunity launch
* **9,479 active email subscribers** (June 2026), growing by 3,457 in the previous 30 days
* **33.0% open rate, 6.7% click rate, 98.3% delivery, 0.3% unsubscribe** across 21 campaigns and 31,503 emails
* Largest single send: a free translation jobs class announcement to 8,190 subscribers
* **5,909 website users and 15,920 views** in the 90 days to August 2026, with a 2 minute average engagement time; the Join page alone drew 14,687 views at a 22% bounce rate; over 90% of the audience in Tanzania
* Applied to host a session at Innovation Week Tanzania 2026 (UNDP, National Planning Commission, COSTECH), framing Tanzanian freelancers earning foreign currency as a services export sector

## What I learned

Transparency is the community glue. I share real costs, real timelines and real constraints, and treat early members as co builders. Emails written like personal letters, with Swahili phrases woven in naturally, outperform newsletter style sends. And insider framing (giving early members behind the scenes access) builds loyalty before the product is polished.
